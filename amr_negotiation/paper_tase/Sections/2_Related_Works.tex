\section{Related Work}\label{sec:rel}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
Resolving head-on encounters in narrow, bay-equipped corridors draws on several research threads that have largely developed in isolation. This section reviews four strands, covering (i)~reactive local planners and their deadlock-breaking extensions, (ii)~centralized and decentralized traffic management, zone control, and multi-agent path finding (MAPF), (iii)~negotiation and mechanism design, including karma-based credit economies, and (iv)~learned negotiators and the benchmarks used to evaluate multi-robot navigation. For each, this section identifies the assumptions that break down when a corridor admits only one robot at a time and yielding requires a costly reverse maneuver into a discrete pull-in bay. Table~\ref{tab:relwork} compares the closest corridor and give-way studies. The related-work search covered IEEE Xplore, the ACM Digital Library, OpenAlex, and arXiv (2015--2026) with the keywords \emph{narrow corridor}, \emph{give way}, \emph{multi-robot deadlock}, \emph{right of way}, \emph{negotiation}, and \emph{mechanism design}, followed by backward and forward citation tracing.

\subsection{Deadlock in Symmetric and Constrained Navigation}
Local reactive planners such as the Dynamic Window Approach \cite{fox1997dwa}, Velocity Obstacles \cite{fiorini1998motion}, RVO/ORCA \cite{vandenberg2011orca}, and decentralized CBFs \cite{wang2017safety} achieve scalable collision avoidance in open spaces, yet stall in symmetric head-on bottlenecks: ORCA clusters lock up \cite{dergachev2021distributed}, and CBF barrier gradients cancel at undesired equilibria \cite{grover2023before}. Grover et al. \cite{grover2023before} formally characterize the equilibrium sets of CBF deadlocks; Zheng and Li \cite{zheng2023deadlock} perturb nonholonomic controls to escape symmetric stalls; Dergachev and Yakovlev \cite{dergachev2021distributed} switch stalled ORCA clusters to local MAPF; and Chandra et al. \cite{chandra2025deadlock,chandra2026social} cast doorway and hallway encounters as ``social mini-games'' resolved via discrete-time CBFs. Without explicit messages, Alghamdi and Park \cite{alghamdi2025opinion} let robots infer from observed motion whether to proceed or yield in a narrow corridor, and Trinh et al. \cite{trinh2015goahead} recognize and resolve robot--human conflicts in narrow passages. However, these methods break deadlocks by adjusting speed, shifting sideways, or waiting, in spaces where robots can eventually pass. When a corridor is narrower than two robot bodies, neither robot can pass until one selects a discrete pull-in bay behind it, reverses into that bay, parks clear of the main lane, and waits for the oncoming robot to clear the aisle.

\subsection{Traffic Management, Zone Control, and MAPF}
In structured warehouses, multi-robot coordination is predominantly centralized \cite{fragapane2021planning}. Classical Automated Guided Vehicle (AGV) zone control partitions aisles into mutually exclusive blocks governed by Petri nets or geometric locks \cite{fanti2002event}, and dynamic resource reservation refines these locks at run time \cite{verma2024traffic}; this paradigm is represented by the first-come \texttt{reservation} baseline in this work. Graph-based MAPF solvers, including CBS \cite{sharon2015cbs}, PIBT \cite{okumura2022pibt}, LaCAM \cite{okumura2023lacam}, safe-interval path planning (SIPP) \cite{phillips2011sipp}, and narrow-corridor extensions \cite{experience2024narrow,macga2025,singh2023conflict}, treat pull-in bays as graph vertices, while kinodynamic planners \cite{wen2022clmapf,dbecbs2025} and industrial traffic frameworks such as Open-RMF \cite{openrmf}, VDA 5050 \cite{vda5050}, and hierarchical zone managers \cite{hiertraffic2023} arbitrate schedules centrally. Precedence-preserving execution protocols \cite{cap2016provably,mannucci2019provably,honig2019persistent} further maintain safety under timing delays or unreliable links once a central priority graph is fixed. Decentralized and semi-decentralized AGV coordination has also been studied in this journal: prioritized planning, in which robots plan in a fixed priority order \cite{cap2015prioritized}, decentralized control of warehouse AGV fleets \cite{draganjac2016decentralized}, ensemble coordination \cite{digani2015ensemble}, and decentralized path planning with supervisory coordination \cite{proia2026decentralized}, while distributed Petri-net control avoids collisions and deadlocks without a central controller \cite{zhou2017collision}; VDA~5050 interoperability across vendors remains an open industrial problem \cite{vanduijkeren2023industrial}. These methods share a pre-computed route graph and resolve conflicts by scheduling access to its vertices and edges. In contrast, this paper studies decentralized encounter resolution without pre-computed joint schedules, and explicitly penalizes the slower reverse kinematics that symmetric graph edge weights ignore.

\subsection{Negotiation, Mechanism Design, and Karma}
Decentralized conflict resolution through explicit rules and messaging dates back to Kato et al. \cite{kato1992coordinating} and Shoham and Tennenholtz's offline social laws \cite{shoham1995social}, alongside local path-negotiation protocols \cite{purwin2008theory} and connected-vehicle bottleneck etiquette \cite{bottleneckiv2023}. In automated driving, \emph{rulebooks} specify behavior as a lexicographically ordered hierarchy of rules \cite{censi2019rulebooks,collin2020safety}; the two rulebook policies in this work follow that formalism. Market-based robotics allocates tasks through cost bids \cite{gerkey2002sold}, and auctions allocate intersection right of way \cite{rey2021online}, whereas reservation-based intersection management \cite{dresner2008multiagent} is the natural precedent for corridor locking. Simple ``lower-bid-yields'' or direct side-payment schemes assume honest reporting. Algorithmic mechanism design \cite{nisan2001algorithmic} via Vickrey--Clarke--Groves (VCG) pivot payments \cite{vickrey1961counterspeculation,clarke1971multipart,groves1973incentives} makes truthful cost reporting a dominant strategy. No mechanism is simultaneously efficient, dominant-strategy truthful, and budget balanced for general quasi-linear preferences \cite{green1977characterization}, and with Bayesian incentive compatibility and individual rationality, budget balance rules out efficiency \cite{myerson1983efficient}. VCG and other truthful mechanisms have been applied to MAPF as combinatorial auctions \cite{amir2015mapfauction}, to strategic agents in social navigation \cite{chandra2023socialmapf}, to scalable MAPF mechanism design \cite{friedrich2024scalable}, and to online prioritization of competing robots that may misreport urgency \cite{das2021omcorp}; Gautier et al. \cite{gautier2022negotiated} resolve path conflicts among non-cooperative robots with a modified combinatorial VCG auction. These studies operate on route graphs with reliable message delivery and do not model the cost of reversing into a refuge. For repeated right-of-way interactions where monetary billing is undesirable, Censi et al. \cite{censi2019karma} and Elokda et al. \cite{elokda2024karma,elokda2025carma} introduce self-contained ``karma'' credit economies, including for traffic bottlenecks \cite{elokda2025carma}. Communication-impairment surveys and delay-tolerant protocols \cite{commfail2021,gielis2022critical} highlight packet drops and staleness as critical deployment bottlenecks, motivating the safeguard ablation at up to 99\% loss presented here.

\subsection{Learned Negotiators and Multi-Robot Benchmarks}
Deep reinforcement learning has been applied to decentralized collision avoidance \cite{chen2017cadrl,long2018towards,fan2020distributed} and to multi-agent path finding \cite{sartoretti2019primal,li2020graph}, PPO-based multi-agent methods \cite{yu2022mappo} have become standard baselines, and constrained corridor give-way tasks include VMAS \texttt{give\_way} \cite{vmasgiveway,bettini2023heterogeneous} and perceptual obstacle hallucination in narrow hallways \cite{park2023hallucination}. The closest benchmark to this work is MARLIN \cite{marlin2024}, which evaluates language-based inter-robot negotiation guiding MARL in single- and two-slot corridors, on physical robots as well as in simulation. Other language-model work covers multi-agent doorway dialogue (GameChat \cite{gamechat2025}) and centralized deadlock recovery (preprint \cite{garg2024llm}). Standardized navigation testbeds such as BARN \cite{xiao2022barn} and SocNavBench \cite{biswas2022socnavbench}, together with evaluation guidelines for social navigation \cite{francis2025principles}, have accelerated reproducible evaluation in cluttered and social spaces, while Gazebo \cite{gazebosim} provides ROS~2 physics. Each of these studies evaluates one or two policy families. To our knowledge, no prior benchmark combines rule-based, lock-based, market-based, mechanism-design, and learned yield policies on the same nonholonomic bay geometry, under strategic misreporting and lossy communication, with seed-paired 2D-to-Gazebo validation (Table~\ref{tab:relwork}).
