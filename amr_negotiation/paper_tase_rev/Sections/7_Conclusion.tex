\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conc}
This paper presented an open dual-simulator benchmark for head-on AMR encounters in narrow corridors with discrete pull-in bays, running one 20\,Hz agent controller across $123{,}024$ 2D episodes and 496 seed-paired ROS~2 / Gazebo Harmonic runs. Communicating policies eliminate the $6.2\%$ deadlock rate and $\ge 3.6\times$ delay penalty of the zero-communication reactive design; randomized backoff raises completion but not efficiency. Kinematic cost bidding matches the better forced-yielder reference in symmetric corridors and cuts heterogeneous urgency-weighted excess delay to $0.04$\,s; it stays ahead of the rulebooks up to random bid error $\sigma=0.3$ in a single bay and $0.35$ in heterogeneous fleets, against a measured 2D planner error of $\sigma\approx0.07$. For self-interested robots, Clarke-pivot payments remove any predicted gain from scaling bids or misreporting private urgency, at the cost of a $24$--$37\%$ ledger surplus and vulnerability to joint bid shading. For cooperative fleets, a cumulative-delay credit with $\lambda$ chosen on held-out sequences reaches Jain fairness $0.992$ at $3.8\%$ extra delay. Collisions stayed at zero up to $99\%$ independent loss and under bursty loss and blackouts, but this comes from the local Lidar and timer layer, to which robots often fall back; attaching bay lists to every message removes incomplete-map penalties. In Gazebo with ground-truth localization, $97.0\%$ of 406 negotiating runs reproduce the 2D yield choice and straight-corridor excess delays match within $1.0$\,s, while T-junction delays do not transfer. Code, worlds, study scripts, and per-episode data are released at \url{https://github.com/briandeegan82/multi-agent-robots}.
