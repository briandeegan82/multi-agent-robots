Three broader lessons emerge. First, \emph{broadcasting snapshots with attached bay lists outperforms handshakes}: bay lists remove incomplete-map penalties and decision disagreements at no cost, which makes \texttt{commit} unnecessary, whereas handshakes and Lidar-gated map exchange add delay. Second, \emph{the value of cost bidding depends on the fleet objective and on random estimation error}, while systematic speed-limit error barely matters. Third, \emph{simplified multi-robot delay models break down before 2D kinematics do}: two-robot corridor delays transfer to Gazebo, but convoys and T-junctions introduce serial waits and timing races that additive delay sums miss.
\subsection{Limitations and Future Scope}
This study has several clear boundaries that point to directions for future work.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Simulation-only evaluation and ground-truth localization.} All experiments ran in 2D and Gazebo simulation (6 to 10 runs per Gazebo condition) with ground-truth pose; localization drift, wheel slip, and TurtleBot3 hardware remain to be tested.
\item \emph{Matched motion model and non-bid cheating.} \cb\ and \texttt{clarke} use the simulator's motion model. Random bid error up to $\sigma=1$ and systematic $\pm30\%$ speed-limit error were tested in 2D, but hardware calibration error, strategic bay hiding, robot-ID spoofing in \texttt{commit} tie-breaks, ledger redistribution, and voluntary participation are not evaluated.
\item \emph{Baselines, perception, and liveness.} The zero-communication baselines are \texttt{reactive} and randomized exponential backoff; continuous planners such as ORCA, RVO, or CBFs with deadlock escape~\cite{zheng2023deadlock} were not implemented, and reservation with off-exit-path staging could not be tested with the current leave maneuver. \texttt{sim2d} models no Lidar detection noise or dropout, temporary mutual yields ($14$--$24\%$ of runs under extreme loss) have no bound on resolution time, and lossy Gazebo runs are not exact pairs of their 2D runs (noisy-bid runs are, once noise is keyed per episode). The better forced yielder is a decision-level reference, not a globally optimal trajectory.
\item \emph{Simplified layouts and decision latency.} The T-junction approximates a side corridor as a $2$\,m-deep bay, \texttt{explore} discovers bays by geometric radius rather than SLAM, the map and blocked-bay sweeps use 10 seeds per condition, and \texttt{learned} is a tabular classifier rather than deep MARL. Every policy here decides in under a millisecond; language-model negotiators~\cite{marlin2024,gamechat2025,garg2024llm} add multi-second inference while robots move and need dedicated asynchronous protocols.
\end{itemize}
